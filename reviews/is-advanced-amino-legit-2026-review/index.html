What is Advanced Amino Formula, and what is included?

Advanced Amino Formula is a tablet supplement sold by Advanced Bionutritionals through ClickBank. The vendor positions its amino acid blend for muscle support, strength, energy, and post-workout recovery. These are seller claims, not findings from a trial of this product.

The label shows five tablets per serving and 30 servings (150 tablets) per bottle. A serving provides 25 calories and 5,000 mg of “Advanced Amino Formula,” a blend of L-leucine, L-valine, L-isoleucine, L-lysine HCl, L-phenylalanine, L-threonine, L-methionine, and L-tryptophan. Individual amounts are not given; daily value for the blend is not established. Other ingredients: vegetable stearic acid, silica, and cellulose coating.

The vendor calls the blend eight essential amino acids. NIH lists nine for adults, including histidine. The sales page disputes histidine’s status, which conflicts with NIH’s list. Ask the seller or a clinician about the discrepancy rather than assuming the formula supplies every essential amino acid.

The reviewed sales page and label do not state a daily schedule. Serving size is not dosing advice; check the bottle and seller instructions.

How Advanced Amino Formula is intended to work

The vendor says essential amino acids provide building blocks for protein and presents the blend for muscle strength, mass, energy, stamina, and post-activity recovery. Broader page claims include ligaments, mood, skin, and hair. These remain vendor claims; the reviewed page did not cite a clinical trial of this product.

The vendor contrasts its blend with whey and BCAAs and makes a numeric claim about whey conversion that we could not assess from the page, so we do not repeat it as fact. Diet, training, and each amino acid’s actual dose all matter.

What independent sources say about the key ingredients

Leucine and the three BCAAs

Leucine, isoleucine, and valine are branched-chain amino acids. NIH’s Office of Dietary Supplements says leucine may stimulate muscle protein synthesis, but finds BCAA performance evidence inconsistent and not consistently better than adequate high-quality protein for muscle or recovery. A meta-analysis in older adults found higher muscle protein fractional synthesis with leucine, but no significant difference in lean or leg lean mass. This mixed ingredient-level evidence does not establish a benefit from this product or its undisclosed leucine dose.

The broader essential amino acid blend

NIH explains that essential amino acids come from food or supplements and adequate dietary protein supports muscle protein synthesis. ODS notes many ingredient combinations have not been studied as finished products. Because this label gives one combined 5,000 mg figure, buyers cannot compare each ingredient dose with a study. Ingredient evidence is not evidence for this product or dose.
