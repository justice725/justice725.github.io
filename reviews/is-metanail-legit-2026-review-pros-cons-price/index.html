What is Metanail Complex, and what is included?

The ClickBank Marketplace describes Metanail Complex as an antifungal dropper and supplement offer. The linked vendor page we reviewed presents the product as Metanail Serum Pro, a topical nail-and-foot serum. The page also promotes Metanail Total Cleanse and digital bonus materials in selected bundles. Those extras are not the same thing as additional serum bottles, so compare the itemized cart rather than treating every bundle component as part of the formula.

The sales page describes the serum as a “20-in-1” formula and names witch hazel, Scots pine, horsetail extract, gotu kola, rosemary, Pelargonium graveolens, glycerin, lemon peel extract, aloe vera, organic green tea, hops, vitamins C and E, hyaluronic acid, jojoba seed oil, sage leaf extract and MSM. It calls the ingredients natural and says they are handled under USDA National Organic Program procedures in an FDA-registered and inspected facility. Those are vendor statements, not independent verification of certification or product efficacy.

Important label gap: in the sales page text available for review, we could not verify a complete Supplement Facts or cosmetic ingredient label, concentration amounts, serving size, servings per container, or directions for applying the serum. The page's inclusion of a large ingredient list does not tell a buyer how much of each material is in the bottle. Ask the vendor for a clear current label before purchase, especially if you have sensitivities or need to check interactions.

What the offer includes: The basic package is listed as one Serum Pro. The two- and four-serum bundles list additional Metanail Total Cleanse units and two digital bonuses. Exact bonus descriptions and bundle totals should be confirmed on the live checkout screen.

How Metanail Complex is intended to work

The vendor says the formula supports nails and feet and describes ingredients as moisturizing, nourishing, antioxidant, or supportive of nail integrity. In its copy, glycerin is associated with hydration; rosemary and geranium are described as skin and cuticle moisturizers; and vitamins C and E are linked to collagen and antioxidant roles. These descriptions explain the seller's rationale. They do not demonstrate that the finished serum changes nail appearance, resolves fungal infection, or works within a particular timeframe.

Some seller copy uses strong language about “incredible results” and “record time,” and the page includes success-story testimonials. Testimonials are anecdotes, not controlled evidence, and the seller's own disclaimer says individual results vary. We found no finished-product clinical trial report on the sales page. Do not substitute a cosmetic serum for diagnosis or care of a persistent, painful, discolored, thickened, or separating nail. A clinician can determine whether a nail change is fungal or has another cause.

What independent sources say about the key ingredients

The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements explains that vitamin C participates in collagen production and functions as an antioxidant in the body. Its fact sheet discusses nutrient intake and oral supplements; it does not establish that applying a serum containing an unspecified amount of vitamin C improves nail health.

NIH ODS describes vitamin E as a fat-soluble antioxidant nutrient and notes that research on some health outcomes is mixed. That general nutrient information does not demonstrate a topical benefit for Metanail Complex. The source also notes that supplements can interact with medicines, which is a reminder to discuss products with a healthcare professional when relevant.

The FDA's consumer overview explains that supplement labels should list serving size, servings per container, ingredients and amounts, and that supplements are not approved by FDA for safety and effectiveness before marketing. Metanail's public page, as read here, does not expose a complete quantities panel in its text. Evidence about an ingredient is not evidence about this product, its formulation, or its dose.

Metanail Complex pros and cons

Potential advantages

  • The page positions Serum Pro as a topical format, so it differs from a capsule-only nail supplement.
  • A range of botanical and moisturizing ingredients is named, giving shoppers a starting point for questions about sensitivities.
  • The vendor advertises a 60-day refund window.
  • The seller states that the purchase is a one-time payment rather than a recurring subscription.

Limitations to weigh

  • The public sales text does not give ingredient quantities, a complete label panel, or clear application directions.
  • Prices and savings language are hard to reconcile: package totals appear beside per-bottle prices and additional products.
  • Refund terms require returning the bottles, but the reviewed page does not clearly state who pays return postage or how empty bottles are handled.
  • We did not find independent clinical evidence for the finished product on the vendor page, and the testimonial claims are not proof of typical outcomes.

Metanail Complex FAQ

Is Metanail Complex a supplement or a topical serum?

The ClickBank listing categorizes it under supplements and calls the offer a dropper and supplement. The vendor sales page reviewed primarily presents Metanail Serum Pro as a topical nail and foot serum. Check the exact product name and package contents at checkout.

What ingredients are named, and are amounts available?

The seller names botanicals, glycerin, vitamins C and E, hyaluronic acid, jojoba oil and MSM. We could not verify amounts, serving size, container count, or a complete label from the sales page text.

How much does it cost?

The vendor page lists $79 for one Serum Pro plus shipping and bundle totals of $187 and $294. It also displays lower per-unit promotional amounts alongside bundles containing other products. Confirm the final itemized cost.

Does it have a money-back guarantee?

The vendor advertises 60 days and says bottles must be mailed back. Return-shipping costs and empty-bottle eligibility were not clear in the page text reviewed.

Can it treat nail fungus?

This review cannot establish a treatment effect. The sales page does not provide independent trials for the finished product. Seek medical advice for persistent or worsening nail symptoms.

Verdict: who should consider Metanail Complex?

Metanail Complex may be worth comparing for an adult who specifically wants a topical nail-and-foot serum, understands that the listed ingredients are not quantified on the reviewed page, and is comfortable confirming package details and return logistics directly. Its most useful selling point is its topical presentation and named blend; the strongest reason for caution is the gap between expansive product claims and limited public formula detail.

Skip it if you need a clinically established antifungal treatment, want ingredient amounts before spending money, or cannot accept the return requirements and unclear shipping details. Nail problems can have causes that need diagnosis, so a clinician is the right starting point for persistent changes. No product outcome is guaranteed by this independent summary.
