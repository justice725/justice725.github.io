What is High Blood Pressure by Blue Heron Health News?

The vendor calls its product The Blood Pressure Program and credits Christian Goodman. It is a digital audio-led mind-body exercise program, not a supplement or medication. The sales page lists three guided audios, a detailed PDF, streaming and download access, and lifetime members-area access. It also mentions an optional CD available for production cost.

The required exercise is nine minutes: walk slowly or tap the knees while seated and follow the audio. Two optional tracks are a 22-minute “Emotional Release” exercise and a 24-minute deep-relaxation exercise. The vendor describes these tracks; they are not an independently tested clinical protocol. There is no ingredient panel, serving size, or product-specific clinical trial report on the reviewed page.

How the method is intended to work

The vendor says these exercises use a mind-body connection to encourage relaxation. Its explanation for the walking or tapping exercise is that alternating “LEFT” and “RIGHT” with movement is intended to relax the medulla oblongata and support blood-pressure regulation. It says the optional breathing and relaxation audios address stress and emotional tension. These are vendor explanations, not verified mechanisms for this specific program.

The sales page includes testimonials describing large blood-pressure changes, including medication changes. Testimonials are not controlled evidence and do not predict an individual result. Never stop, reduce, or delay prescribed treatment because of an audio program or an online testimonial; speak with your clinician.

What independent sources say about the method

Slow breathing and relaxation

NIH’s NCCIH reports that a review of 17 studies involving 1,165 participants associated slow breathing with a modest blood-pressure reduction. The studies varied, had short follow-up, and were at high risk of bias; they did not establish effects on stroke or heart attack. NCCIH says people with hypertension should follow their care plan and discuss complementary approaches. This general evidence does not establish the effects of this product.

Blood-pressure care

The American Heart Association says high blood pressure often has no symptoms and advises taking prescribed medicine as directed, monitoring readings, and working with a health professional. Lifestyle measures such as activity, heart-healthy eating, and stress management may help manage blood pressure. That general advice does not prove this program’s specific exercises lower readings. No independent product-specific trial was identified in the vendor material reviewed.
