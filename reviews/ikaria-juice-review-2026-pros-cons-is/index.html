What is Ikaria Juice, and what's included?

Ikaria Juice is marketed on its sales page as Ikaria Lean Belly Juice, a powdered dietary supplement. The vendor's directions say to stir one scoop into a glass of water or another preferred beverage each morning. It is presented as a drink mix rather than a capsule or liquid dropper product. The sales page lists digital bonuses and coaching with qualifying multi-bottle orders; confirm inclusions at checkout.

The listed ingredients include fucoxanthin, Panax ginseng, BioPerine (black-pepper extract), resveratrol, EGCG from green tea, Taraxacum, citrus pectin, and milk thistle. The vendor also names an antioxidant blend containing beet root, hibiscus, strawberry extract, acai extract, African mango extract, black currant extract, and blueberry powder. The page does not provide amounts for each ingredient in the text reviewed, so shoppers who need dose-level detail should inspect the current product label and ask the seller before ordering.

This is the vendor's ingredient list, not evidence that the formula produces a specific outcome. Formulas can change; check the package label.

How Ikaria Juice is intended to work

The vendor says the formula is designed to address ceramides and support metabolism, liver health, energy, and digestion. It presents the ingredients as a blend intended to support those processes. These are the seller's explanations and marketing claims; this review has not independently established that the product causes fat loss, changes metabolism, or delivers the outcomes described in the sales copy.

In practical terms, it is a daily beverage routine. A listed botanical alone does not establish that a finished formula is effective or suitable for everyone.

The official offer page reviewed lists a 30-day supply at $69 per bottle plus shipping, a 90-day supply for $177 total ($59 per bottle), and a 180-day supply for $234 total ($39 per bottle).
