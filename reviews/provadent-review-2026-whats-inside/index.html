What is ProvaDent, and what does it include?

ProvaDent is marketed as a chewable oral supplement, made in the United States with domestic and foreign ingredients, according to the seller. Its ingredient list names five components or groups but omits amounts and the probiotic strain identities.

Organic xylitol

Listed by the seller as a sweetener intended to support a balanced oral environment and fresh breath.

BioFresh™ Clean Complex

A proprietary blend. The vendor does not disclose its component ingredients or their quantities in the sales-page text reviewed.

Cranberry extract

Included by the vendor for potential oral-health support. No extract strength or proanthocyanidin amount is shown.

Purple carrot powder

Listed as a plant ingredient; the vendor does not specify an amount or standardized active component.

Four probiotic strains

The page names a four-strain blend but does not list the strains, viable count, or per-serving dose.

The vendor recommends two tablets daily and describes each bottle as a 30-day supply. The provided product image does not show a legible Supplement Facts panel, so exact ingredient amounts and probiotic strains cannot be confirmed. Three- and six-bottle packages are advertised with two digital bonuses, Beyond the Brush and Tooth or Dare; file formats and page counts are not specified.

How ProvaDent is intended to work

The vendor says ProvaDent is intended to support a balanced oral microbiome. It attributes that aim to the probiotic blend, cranberry extract, BioFresh complex, xylitol, and purple carrot powder. These are seller claims, not established effects for this finished formula.

No product-specific clinical trial appears in the materials reviewed. Testimonials are marketing, not controlled evidence. Do not replace routine dental care or symptom evaluation with a supplement.

What independent sources say about the key ingredients

Oral probiotics. A 2024 review in Frontiers in Microbiology evaluates evidence by bacterial strain and cautions that many products on the market use strains without documented oral-health benefits. The authors say safety or familiarity with a species does not establish efficacy, and strain-specific evidence matters. ProvaDent's sales-page ingredient list does not name its strains or disclose their dose.

Cranberry extract. A review available through PubMed Central describes laboratory findings about cranberry polyphenols and bacterial adhesion, but notes that much oral-health evidence was in vitro and that stronger clinical studies are needed. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health also says research on cranberry for conditions beyond urinary infections is limited. Neither source establishes that ProvaDent's unspecified cranberry dose produces a dental benefit.

Xylitol. A 2024 systematic review on xylitol and dental caries concluded that a preventive effect could not be confirmed with certainty and called for standardized research. Study results about xylitol alone do not tell us how much xylitol ProvaDent contains or what this combination does.

Evidence boundary: Research on an ingredient is not proof of the finished product, its dose, or the results a particular person will experience. The label gaps here make that distinction especially important.

The refund page gives a 60-day return window and says customers pay return postage. It does not clearly state whether empty bottles must be returned or how opened bottles are handled. Ask support if this affects your decision, and keep your order confirmation.

The order flow uses discount and restock messaging. Treat that urgency and testimonials as vendor marketing, not verification. Vendor materials identify Adem Naturals; the refund page lists support@getprovadent.com and 1 (814) 885-4823. ClickBank identifies itself as retailer, not as an endorser.
