Advanced Mitochondrial Formula ingredients: what’s included?

The sales page describes a capsule supplement from Advanced Bionutritionals and recommends two capsules daily. It names niacinamide, D-ribose, and Japanese knotweed/Itadori extract (listed as Polygonum cuspidatum) as its “primary” nutrients. The other named ingredients are pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ), alpha-lipoic acid, BioPerine (black pepper extract), acetyl-L-carnitine, coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), curcumin, quercetin, and magnesium.

That page therefore names eleven ingredients across its two groups, while a separate FAQ calls the formula “10” nutrients. This is an inconsistency in the vendor’s own copy. We could not verify a readable Supplement Facts panel with per-ingredient amounts, capsule serving size, or servings per container from the materials available to us. Those specifics matter: without quantities and the exact form of each ingredient, a reader cannot compare the blend with research doses or calculate price per serving or day. Ask the seller to confirm the current label before ordering.

The main page is a marketing page, not a complete technical label. It presents the ingredients as a complex aimed at energy metabolism and mitochondrial function. That description should not be mistaken for proof that the finished product has been tested in a clinical trial.

How Advanced Mitochondrial Formula is intended to work

The vendor says niacinamide can support NAD-related pathways, D-ribose supplies a building block involved in ATP, and PQQ supports mitochondrial biogenesis signaling. Its copy describes CoQ10 and alpha-lipoic acid as antioxidant-related ingredients, acetyl-L-carnitine as involved in transporting fatty acids, and curcumin and quercetin as plant compounds. These are the seller’s descriptions of intended action, not established outcomes for this specific product.

The promotional page also makes strong statements about energy, mobility, cognition, and healthy aging. We found no product-specific controlled trial described on the page, and testimonials there are not a substitute for controlled evidence. FTC guidance says objective health-benefit claims in advertising need competent and reliable scientific support. A general study of an ingredient cannot establish that this particular formula, at its undisclosed dose, produces a stated result.

What independent sources say about the key ingredients

Niacinamide (vitamin B3): The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements explains that niacin helps the body turn food into energy and that supplement forms include nicotinamide. The fact sheet also notes potential adverse effects and interactions at high intake; the product page does not disclose the formula’s amount. See the NIH ODS Niacin fact sheet.

D-ribose: A PubMed-indexed pilot study evaluated D-ribose in people with chronic fatigue syndrome and fibromyalgia. It was open-label and uncontrolled, so it cannot establish efficacy on its own, and its study population and intake do not show that this commercial blend has the same effect. Read the PubMed abstract.

CoQ10: CoQ10 is one ingredient on the seller’s list, but this page does not provide its quantity. A claim about CoQ10 generally—or evidence from a different dose or population—is not evidence for Advanced Mitochondrial Formula. Ask a clinician or pharmacist about supplements when taking prescription medicine; we did not find a product-specific trial in the supplied sales-page material.

Across all ingredients, evidence about an ingredient is not evidence about this product or its dose. The NIH and PubMed sources above are not endorsements of this brand.

  • The page states a 90-day satisfaction guarantee and says even empty bottles can be returned.

Limitations to weigh

  • Per-ingredient amounts and servings per bottle were not readable in the supplied sales-page text, limiting dose and value comparisons.
  • No product-specific independent clinical trial was identified in the materials reviewed.
  • The single-bottle offer is $79.95; the three- and six-bottle totals require a larger upfront spend.

Red flags and things to verify before you buy

Before checkout, confirm that the supplement facts label matches the blend and provides amounts for every ingredient. The page’s “10 nutrients” FAQ conflicts with its eleven named ingredients. Confirm whether the selected order is a one-time purchase and whether any recurring billing applies; the sales copy reviewed lists packages but does not clearly explain recurring-order terms. No subscription was stated in the package block we reviewed, which is not a guarantee about future checkout terms.

The return language says the guarantee lasts 90 days, accepts empty bottles, and requires the buyer to pay return shipping; other page copy says shipping and handling are refunded, which is not perfectly consistent. Ask customer support what amount is refunded and how to obtain a return authorization. The sales page identifies Advanced Bionutritionals and gives 1-800-791-3395. Its headline and body use forceful claims about feeling younger and guaranteed energy; read such claims critically, since results are not assured by ingredient research alone. We did not search FDA warning-letter records or FTC case records for this company or product, so this review makes no claim about those databases.
