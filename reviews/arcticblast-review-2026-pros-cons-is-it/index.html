What is ArcticBlast and what’s included?

ArcticBlast is marketed as an external-use cooling liquid, not an oral dietary supplement. The product page describes menthol as its main ingredient and lists camphor, aloe barbadensis, arnica montana flower extract, wintergreen oil, St. John’s wort oil and calendula officinalis extract in the formula. Ingredient names are reproduced from the vendor’s sales page; check the package label for the full ingredient list, concentrations and current directions.

The offer also advertises three digital recipe or wellness bonuses with an order: “The Anti-Inflammation Diet,” “Feed Your Joints Back To Life,” and “Longevity Secrets From The Healthiest 100-Year-Olds.” The page describes these as free ebooks. Bonus availability and terms can change, so review the live offer before paying. The vendor additionally advertises free U.S. shipping, which should also be confirmed at checkout.

How ArcticBlast is intended to work

The vendor says menthol creates a cooling sensation followed by warmth, which may temporarily distract from the feeling of occasional muscle or joint discomfort. The sales copy characterizes this as a topical counter-irritant effect. It also describes camphor as a common ingredient in topical analgesics, while the botanical ingredients are presented as part of its formula.

These are product-page descriptions, not independent confirmation that ArcticBlast relieves a specific condition. The page’s application instructions are not fully consistent: one section says 3–4 drops, while its FAQ says 2–3 drops. Follow the current product label rather than relying on an inconsistent web description. Avoid contact with eyes, wash hands after application, and keep the product away from children. The seller says it is not for people under 18 and advises consulting a clinician in relevant health circumstances.
