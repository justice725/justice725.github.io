What is Finessa? Ingredients and what's included

Finessa is presented as a powdered dietary supplement for digestive support. The seller says to mix one scoop into water or another beverage and drink it once each morning. The sales page also advertises three digital bonuses with multi-bottle packages: Anti-Aging Superfoods, Cheat Your Way Slim, and Powerful Detox Smoothies. The page's package cards repeat the bonus labels, but the headline specifically highlights the three- and six-bottle offers. Confirm which bonuses are included with the exact cart selection.

The product label image lists a serving size of 1 scoop and 50 servings per container. It prints amounts for several blends, but not the amount of each individual botanical within those blends. The following transcription keeps those distinctions visible rather than treating a blend total as an ingredient dose.

Supplement Facts shown on the label image

  • Calories: 10; Total carbohydrate: 3 g (<1% DV); Selenium: 1 mcg (2% DV); Sodium: 40 mg (2% DV); Potassium: 10 mg (<1% DV).
  • Polyphenol Blend — 1,000 mg: beet root powder, black currant powder, pomegranate powder, raspberry powder, strawberry juice powder, cranberry powder, blueberry powder, purple carrot powder, and acai berry extract.
  • Gut Health — 755 mg: cinnamon bark, ginger root, cascara sagrada bark (10:1), deglycyrrhizinated licorice root extract, and fructooligosaccharides.
  • Liver Health Blend — 450 mg: dandelion root powder, turmeric root powder, milk thistle powder, and artichoke extract (Cynara scolymus; standardized to 5% cynarin); whole-herb wording appears on the image, and it specifies 80% silymarin (as silybin) from milk thistle seed extract.
  • Probiotic Blend — 52.5 mg: the image says 5 billion CFU at time of manufacture. Listed organisms include L. casei, L. acidophilus, L. rhamnosus, a second L. casei listing, Clostridium butyricum, Streptococcus thermophilus, Bacillus coagulans BG30, Lactobacillus plantarum, Bifidobacterium lactis BL80, and Lactobacillus reuteri LR08. The image gives percentages for some organisms, but not a clear CFU count for each strain.
  • Probiotic Powder — 2 mg: Akkermansia muciniphila AH39.
  • Other ingredients: resistant dextrin, natural flavors, citric acid, sea salt, stevia extract (leaf), and silicon dioxide.

The label notes that the probiotic CFU figure is measured at manufacture, not at the end of shelf life. The image is the vendor's label artwork; this transcription is not an independent laboratory verification.

Serving-count discrepancy: the label image says 50 servings per container, while the sales page describes one bottle as a 30-day supply and the three- and six-bottle packages as 90 and 180 days. Those statements do not line up with one scoop daily. Ask the seller to reconcile the bottle size and serving count before using the quoted per-day figures.

How Finessa is intended to work

The vendor frames the formula around the “gut-liver axis.” Its sales page says the blend is intended to support digestion, bowel regularity, the colon, and bile flow. It describes dandelion, milk thistle, artichoke, and turmeric as a liver-health blend; fiber-like ingredients and botanicals appear in the gut-health blend; and several listed microorganisms make up the probiotic blend.

These are vendor claims, not proven outcomes for Finessa. Blend totals hide individual botanical doses, and the reviewed page presents no independent trial of the finished formula. Ingredient claims do not establish treatment of constipation, bloating, or disease.

What independent sources say about the key ingredients

Probiotics. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements explains that probiotic effects can vary by species and strain, and that not every product labeled as a probiotic has proven benefits. Findings for other strains cannot establish effects of Finessa's blend. Read the NIH ODS Probiotics fact sheet.

Turmeric/curcumin. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) says evidence is not sufficient to definitively conclude that turmeric or curcumin benefits health conditions. NCCIH also notes possible digestive side effects and reports of liver injury with some enhanced-bioavailability products. See NCCIH's turmeric overview.

Milk thistle/silymarin. NCCIH reports that evidence from human studies is not strong enough for definite conclusions, and that liver-disease trial results have been conflicting or limited. This matters because Finessa groups milk thistle into a 450 mg blend rather than specifying its own dose. See NCCIH's milk thistle overview.

Evidence boundary: ingredient research is not evidence that this product works, and it does not validate Finessa's specific doses, mixture, quality, or label. No product-specific independent trial was identified in the materials reviewed.

Finessa pros and cons

Potential advantages

  • The powder format may suit people who prefer a drink mix over capsules.
  • The label gives total amounts for four blends, serving size, serving count, and a list of other ingredients.
  • The vendor advertises a long 180-day money-back period and says used product may still qualify.
  • Three package sizes are listed, with the six-bottle option showing free U.S. shipping.

Limitations to weigh

  • The 50-serving label conflicts with the advertised 30-day supply per bottle.
  • Amounts for individual herbs inside the 450 mg and 755 mg blends are not itemized.
  • No independent clinical trial of the Finessa formula was identified in the materials reviewed.
  • Shipping charges for the one- and three-bottle options, return-shipping rules, and recurring-order terms are not clear on the main sales page.

Red flags and things to verify before you buy

The most concrete issue is the serving-count mismatch: 50 servings on the label versus a stated 30-day supply for one bottle. It may reflect different serving assumptions or package information, but the page does not explain it. Ask support what one bottle contains and how long it is intended to last.

The sales page promises a 180-day refund, including when the order has been used, and says to contact support. It does not clearly explain who pays return shipping, whether there are other conditions, or how to start the refund. The support email shown on the page is support@myfinessa.com. A specific legal company name, street address, or phone number is not apparent in the sales-page material reviewed. Check the linked policies and keep the order receipt.

I did not find clear auto-ship or auto-renew wording on the main offer page. That is not confirmation that recurring billing cannot apply; inspect the order summary and terms before submitting payment. The one- and three-bottle cards say "+Shipping," without a dollar amount, while six bottles are listed with free U.S. shipping. The page also uses "while stock lasts" and says prices may not stay low. Treat these as seller claims, not proof of a deadline or shortage.

Finally, the page makes broad claims about body shape, energy, metabolism, and digestion. Those are vendor statements, not independent findings. The FTC says health-related advertising claims need adequate substantiation and must not mislead. Its Health Products Compliance Guidance explains the general standard.
